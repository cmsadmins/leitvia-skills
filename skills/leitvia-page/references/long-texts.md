# Long texts

Details for `leitvia-page`, `leitvia-news` and `leitvia-migrate`. A page or post of about 1500 words or more is read in parts: readers jump to what they need and come back. Give it a form that helps them, whoever wrote the words.

- **Table of contents** near the top: the block `leitvia/table-of-contents` (`levels` `[2, 3]` by default). It lists the headings when the page renders, so it needs no edits later. One per page. From about 2500 words set `display` to `companion`: a chapter button with the section being read stays at the bottom edge once the list is scrolled away.
- **Parts from about 6000 words:** split the post with `core/nextpage` into parts of 2000 to 3000 words at natural breaks (stage, place, day). Each part starts with a heading; the theme shows the navigation between the parts. One table of contents at the top of part 1 is enough: from Leitvia Studio 1.3.35 it shows at the top of every part, groups the headings under Part 1, Part 2 and so on, marks the part being read and links headings of other parts to their part with the anchor. `scope` `part` lists only the part being read. Studio 1.3.34 and earlier list every heading with a bare anchor that leads nowhere in other parts: there, keep the post on one page. A link in the text to `#anchor` of another part needs the address of that part: from Studio 1.3.36 a write gives it the path of that part once the post is published with pretty permalinks, `leitvia_anchor_other_part` names links that still lead nowhere (stored blocks, drafts) and `leitvia_anchor_missing` anchors that exist in no part, `leitvia/audit-site` reports both. Subheadings come from place names and sentences of the text; a new wording is a suggestion in the report. Compare the text per part and for the whole post.
- **Fact box** at the top, before the first section: the key facts of the topic as a short list or a table of two columns inside a group with the style `note`. For a travel destination these are country, season, where to stay or park, costs; for a guide what you need and how long it takes. Facts only from a source you can name.
- **A heading at least every 300 words.** An `h2` that names its section, `h3` below it; never skip a level. Turn a long paragraph into a list only where the text already counts things up.
- **Reading breaks** between sections: a tip of the author, an image or a quote, never two of them in a row and never in place of a heading.
- **Author tip:** the pattern `leitvia/content-author-tip` (group style `tip`, round portrait in an accent ring, heart before the eyebrow "My tip", the tip as a quote, the name in italics). Create it once as a synced pattern with the real portrait and name of the author (`leitvia/create-pattern` with `synced: true`, see [leitvia-pattern](../../leitvia-pattern/SKILL.md)) and place it every three to five sections of a long guide. The paragraphs named `Tip` and `Name` carry pattern overrides, so every use has its own text: `<!-- wp:block {"ref":<ID>,"content":{"Tip":{"content":"<tip>"},"Name":{"content":"<name>"}}} /-->`. Without an entry for a name the synced default stays. Never copy the tip as loose blocks to change its text, and never add a frame, a left line or a radius of your own. Without a real portrait and name of the author, leave the tip out.
- **Link lists:** more than six targets are cards with an image, or the pattern `leitvia/teaser-icon-tiles` (icon tiles, one link over the whole tile), never a run of headings. Use the own icons of the brand (`add-icon`, Pro skills) where the site has them, not core icons.
- **Trip reports with stages:** a post whose stages read "Day 3: A to B" gets the route block from the catalog (`leitvia/list-building-blocks`) at the top, when the site has it.
- **Many images in a row:** a section with more than three images uses a scroll gallery block when `leitvia/list-blocks` lists one (images next to a box that stays in view), otherwise a `core/gallery`. Never a long run of single image blocks.

Check before you report: count the words between two headings in the outline from `leitvia/get-page-outline`; no stretch over 300 words.
